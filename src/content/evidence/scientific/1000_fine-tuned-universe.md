The universe exhibits extraordinary precision in its fundamental constants. If any of these values were even slightly different, life would be impossible. This precision points to an intelligent Creator who designed the cosmos with purpose.

> "If any of the dozens of 'dials' were moved by a hair's breadth, life would be impossible."
> — Dr. Stephen Meyer

## Key Constants

### The Numbers That Define Reality

Fine-tuning proponents point to several constants that appear remarkably precise:

- **Expansion rate:** Proponents argue it is precise to 1 in 10^60. If faster, galaxies would fly apart. If slower, the universe would collapse.
- **Gravity:** Proponents argue it is precise to 1 in 10^40. If stronger, stars would burn too fast. If weaker, no stars would form at all.
- **Cosmological constant:** This is where it gets interesting. The 10^120 figure actually represents a *discrepancy* between the observed value and theoretical predictions—a problem physicists call "the worst prediction in physics." This isn't precision but an unexplained puzzle.

:::info
These figures are contested by many physicists. Some argue the probability estimates are exaggerated, while others propose natural explanations. The debate continues, but many see the precision as evidence for intentional design.
:::

## What Does This Mean?

There are two main explanations for this fine-tuning:

1. **Multiverse:** Perhaps there are many universes, and we just happen to live in one that supports life. However, this raises questions about where all these universes come from.
2. **Intelligent Design:** The precision points to a Creator who intentionally designed the universe to support life.

:::info
Either way—multiple universes or one Designer—something exists beyond the physical universe. The evidence points to a cause that is not subject to the physical laws it created.
:::

## The Catholic Connection

This scientific discovery aligns beautifully with Catholic teaching. The Church has always taught that God created the universe intentionally, with purpose and design. The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that God created "not from any necessity nor from chance but from free will" (CCC 280).

Modern cosmology has only confirmed what faith has always proclaimed: "In the beginning, God created the heavens and the earth." The precision we discover in the cosmos isn't an accident—it's an invitation to know the One who crafted every detail with love.
