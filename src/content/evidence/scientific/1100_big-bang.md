For centuries, scientists believed the universe was eternal. It had always existed with no beginning. Then everything changed.

In 1927, a Belgian physicist made a startling discovery. He found that galaxies are moving away from each other. The universe is expanding. This meant something remarkable. If you press play on that expansion going backward, you reach a point where everything began.

Today, scientists confirm the universe is about 13.8 billion years old. Everything started in one moment. All matter, energy, space, and time came into existence then. This event is called the Big Bang.

## What Genesis Said First

This scientific discovery matches what the Bible said thousands of years ago. The book of Genesis begins with these words: ["In the beginning, God created the heavens and the earth"](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis+1%3A1&version=NABRE). This simple statement affirms that the universe had a starting point. It did not always exist.

The Catholic Church has taught this for centuries. The Church calls this creation "ex nihilo." This Latin phrase means "out of nothing." God did not make the universe from existing materials. God created everything from pure nothingness.

This idea might sound strange. How can something come from nothing? Let us think about this more carefully.

## The Nothing Problem

Physicist Lawrence Krauss wrote a book titled "A Universe from Nothing." He argues the universe came from true nothingness. But there is a problem with this claim.

Krauss's "nothing" is not really nothing. It is a quantum vacuum. This vacuum has energy. It has physical laws. It has quantum fields that can produce particles. That is not "nothing" in any meaningful sense.

True nothing means the absence of anything at all. No energy. No laws. No space. No time. Nothing. Such a state cannot produce anything. Yet the universe exists. Therefore, something must have caused it.

The Big Bang requires a cause outside the universe. This cause cannot be made of matter or energy. It cannot exist in space or time. It must be immaterial. It must be timeless. It must be powerful beyond our imagination.

## The Kind of Cause Required

Think about what the cause of the universe must be like.

It cannot be physical. Physical things exist in space and time. The cause existed before space and time began. So it must be non-physical, or immaterial.

It must be timeless. Time began at the Big Bang. The cause existed before time existed. Therefore, the cause exists outside of time entirely.

It must be unimaginably powerful. Creating everything from nothing requires infinite power. Nothing less could bring all matter and energy into existence.

## A Priest Who Predicted the Big Bang

Here is something many people do not know. The Big Bang theory was proposed by a Catholic priest.

His name was Georges Lemaître. He lived from 1894 to 1966. He was both a Catholic priest and a physicist. In 1927, he published the theory that would become the Big Bang.

The Church has never been opposed to scientific truth. The Church has always encouraged investigation of the natural world. Lemaître himself was careful not to connect his science directly to his faith. He saw them as different domains. But his faith did not conflict with his science. They fit together.

The Catholic Church teaches that God created the universe purposefully. The world is not a part of God. This idea is called pantheism. The Church rejects it. God is distinct from creation. Creation reflects God's goodness and wisdom.

## Science and Faith Working Together

The Big Bang shows something important. Science and faith are not enemies.

Science describes how the universe works. Faith asks why there is something rather than nothing. These are different questions. They do not have to conflict.

For centuries, some assumed the universe was eternal. They thought science proved no beginning was necessary. Now science shows a beginning did occur. This aligns with what many religious traditions have taught.

The astronomer Edwin Hubble provided key evidence. He showed that galaxies are moving away from us. This is called Hubble's Law. The farther away a galaxy, the faster it moves. This expansion points backward to a single starting point.

Another key piece of evidence is the cosmic microwave background. This is faint radiation throughout the universe. It is the afterglow of the initial fireball. It was predicted by the Big Bang theory and later discovered.

The abundance of light elements also supports the theory. In the early universe, simple elements formed in specific amounts. These predictions match what we observe today.

## Looking Up at the Stars

When you look at the night sky, you are seeing light that traveled billions of years. You are looking at a universe that had a beginning. You are witnessing something extraordinary.

The Apostle Paul wrote something interesting. He said God's invisible qualities are clearly seen. They are understood from the things that are made. The order and design of creation point toward a creator. This comes from [Romans 1:20](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Romans+1%3A20&version=NABRE).

Whether you believe in God or not, the Big Bang raises profound questions. Why is there something rather than nothing? Why does the universe have laws that allow for life? Why does anything exist at all?

These questions do not have easy answers. But the evidence points toward a beginning. That beginning requires explanation. The universe did not create itself. Something outside the universe brought it into being.

What that something is remains open for exploration.
