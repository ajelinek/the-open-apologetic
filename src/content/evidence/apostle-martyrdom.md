The apostles went from hiding in fear after Jesus's crucifixion to boldly proclaiming His resurrection—and ultimately, most of them died as martyrs. Why would they do this? Why would they suffer and die for a lie?

## Historical Records of Martyrdom

The early church historians provide accounts:

- **Peter:** Crucified upside down in Rome (c. 64-67 AD)
- **Paul:** Beheaded in Rome (c. 64-67 AD)
- **James:** Stoned to death in Jerusalem (c. 44 AD)
- **Andrew:** Crucified in Greece (c. 60-70 AD)
- **Thomas:** Martyred in India (c. 72 AD)
- **Bartholomew:** Martyred in Armenia (c. 71 AD)

## Why This Matters

Christianity didn't begin with a book—it began with witnesses. These men claimed to have seen Jesus:
- After His resurrection
- After His ascension
- Performing miracles
- Teaching with authority

If they were lying, why would they:
- Give up their lives for a falsehood?
- Suffer persecution, torture, and death?
- Maintain their testimony until the end?

:::quote
"Men do not die for what they know is a lie. The willingness of the apostles to die for their faith is powerful evidence that they truly believed what they proclaimed."
:::

## The Catholic Connection

The apostles passed on their witness to the bishops who succeeded them, creating an unbroken chain of testimony that continues in the Catholic Church today. When the Pope and bishops teach with authority, they exercise the same apostolic office the original apostles held.
