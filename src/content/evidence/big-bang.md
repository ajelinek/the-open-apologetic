For centuries, scientists believed the universe was eternal—it had always existed. Then in 1927, Georges Lemaître proposed what would become the Big Bang theory. Today, it's confirmed: the universe had a beginning.

## What the Big Bang Tells Us

The Big Bang theory reveals that:
- The universe began approximately 13.8 billion years ago
- All matter, energy, space, and time came into existence at that moment
- The universe is expanding from an extremely hot, dense initial state

This is precisely what Genesis has proclaimed for thousands of years: "In the beginning, God created the heavens and the earth."

## Something from Nothing?

Atheist physicist Lawrence Krauss wrote a book titled "A Universe from Nothing." But here's the problem: his "nothing" isn't really nothing—it's a quantum vacuum with energy and physical laws. That's not "nothing" in any meaningful sense.

True "nothing"—the absence of anything at all—cannot produce anything. Yet the universe exists. Therefore, there must be a cause outside the universe.

:::info
The Big Bang requires a cause that is not itself part of space, time, matter, or energy. That cause must be immaterial and powerful beyond comprehension—the definition of God.
:::

## The Catholic Connection

The Big Bang theory was actually proposed by a Catholic priest, Monsignor Georges Lemaître, in 1927. The Church has never been opposed to scientific truth—quite the opposite. The Church affirms that God created the universe ex nihilo (out of nothing).
