In the Catholic Church, there are over 250 canonized saints whose bodies have been found to be incorrupt—that is, preserved from normal decomposition. This phenomenon has been documented, examined, and verified.

## What Is Incorruptibility?

Incorrpution is the preservation of a body from decomposition, sometimes centuries after death. The bodies are not mummified or artificially preserved—they simply didn't decay.

### Notable Examples

- **St. Francis of Assisi:** Died 1226, body found incorrupt in 1900
- **St. Catherine of Siena:** Died 1380, body incorrupt
- **St. John Vianney:** Died 1859, body remains incorrupt
- **St. Padre Pio:** Died 1968, body shows no signs of decay

## Scientific Examination

Doctors and scientists have examined these bodies:
- No evidence of artificial preservation
- Tissue remains flexible
- Natural decay process simply didn't occur

:::quote
"The incorruptibles are a preview of the resurrection that awaits us—a promise that the body, like the soul, has eternal destiny."
:::

## The Catholic Connection

The Catholic Church teaches that at the end of time, God will resurrect our bodies and unite them with our souls. The incorruptible saints are seen as a "preview" of this resurrection. They're not worshipped—the Church honors them as holy men and women who now pray for us before God's throne.
