In Catholic tradition and popular devotion, there are over 250 saints whose bodies have been found to be incorrupt—that is, preserved from normal decomposition. This phenomenon has been documented, examined, and verified by medical professionals over centuries. It's important to note that this count comes from popular Catholic devotion rather than official Church certification.

## What Is Incorruptibility?

Incorruptibility is the preservation of a body from decomposition, sometimes centuries after death. Unlike mummification or artificial preservation, these bodies show no signs of the normal decay process. The Church sees this as a sign of holiness—a glimpse of what our resurrected bodies might be like.

### Notable Examples

- **St. Catherine of Siena:** Died 1380. Her head is preserved and displayed in Siena, while her body rests in Rome and her foot in Venice.
- **St. John Vianney:** Died 1859. His heart remains incorrupt and is preserved in a reliquary.
- **St. Padre Pio:** Died 1968. When exhumed in 2008 for investigation, his body was found in remarkable condition, though the preservation remains a subject of scholarly discussion.
- **St. Francis of Assisi:** Died 1226. His remains have been examined over the centuries, with bones displayed in recent years. The historical record shows various examinations of his remains.

## Scientific Examination

Doctors and scientists have examined these bodies with interest:

- Some show remarkable preservation that defies normal expectations
- The degree of preservation varies among different saints
- Scholarly debate continues about the precise nature of some preservation cases

"The incorruptibles are a preview of the resurrection that awaits us—a promise that the body, like the soul, has eternal destiny."

## The Catholic Connection

The Catholic Church teaches that at the end of time, God will resurrect our bodies and unite them with our souls. This teaching comes from Scripture and is detailed in the Catechism (CCC 1016-1017). The incorruptible saints are seen as a "preview" of this resurrection—an early taste of what awaits all of us who follow Christ.

The Church doesn't worship these saints. Instead, she honors them as holy men and women who now pray for us before God's throne. Their preserved bodies serve as a reminder that our physical existence matters to God—that He doesn't abandon His creation but restores it to perfection.

## What This Means for You

The incorruptible saints point to something bigger than themselves. They remind us that our bodies aren't just temporary vessels to be discarded. They're part of who we are, and God intends to redeem them completely.

When you think about your own body—flawed, aging, imperfect—know that it's loved by the same God who preserved His saints. Your resurrection is already secured, even if the details remain a mystery.

Maybe the question isn't whether your body will be restored, but how you're living now in light of that future reality.
