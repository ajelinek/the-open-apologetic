DNA is often called the "code of life." But what does that actually mean? When scientists began studying DNA in the 1950s, they discovered something revolutionary: DNA functions exactly like a digital code.

## The Digital Code

DNA uses only four chemical bases—adenine (A), cytosine (C), guanine (G), and thymine (T)—arranged in sequences. This is essentially a four-letter alphabet that stores information:

- **One DNA molecule** contains 3 billion base pairs
- **The human body** has 37 trillion cells
- **Each cell** contains 6 feet of DNA

The information density is staggering. As information theorist William Dembski notes, specifying even a single protein sequence requires more information than in the entire works of Shakespeare.

## Where Does Information Come From?

In our experience, information always comes from a mind. Books come from authors. Software comes from programmers. Code comes from coders.

DNA contains information—specifically, the instructions for building and maintaining a living organism. Where did this information come from?

:::quote
"DNA is not merely a chemical molecule; it is a information storage system. And as far as we know, information only comes from intelligence."
:::
