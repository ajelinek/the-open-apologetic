# Rules: Evidence Content Creation

This document defines specific and prescriptive rules for evidence article creation and quality enforcement.

## Target Audience

- **General audience**: Everyone
- **Specifically**: Laypeople, atheists, agnostics, and spiritual individuals who do not believe in God or Jesus Christ
- **Reading level**: National reading level (6th-7th grade, approximately age 11-12)

## Document Structure

Evidence articles must follow this structure:

1. **Hook**: Start with a universally relatable question OR an aha fact/statement
2. **Context**: Provide just enough background to understand
3. **Content**: Present the theological material in accessible language
4. **Connection**: Link back to everyday life and real experiences
5. **End**: Close with reflection. No call to action. This is information sharing.

## Word Count

- **Required range**: 600–1,400 words
- Run word count before finalizing
- Revise and re-count if outside range

## Formatting

### Subheadings

- Use for longer pieces
- Keep descriptive and concise

### Text Emphasis

- **Minimize** bold, italics, and other text decoration
- Use only when truly needed for clarity
- Avoid decorative formatting entirely

### Bible Verse References

- Include ONLY when directly quoting Scripture or providing significant verification value
- Format: `[Reference](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis+12%3A10-20)`
- Theologian-expert must verify all verse references

### External Links

- Use external links sparingly and only for verification purposes
- Links should help readers verify claims or explore further
- Do not overload with links—include only authoritative sources
- Format: `[text](url)`
- Ensure all external links are functional and point to reputable sources

### Sources / References

- All sources go in front matter as references
- Include author, title, publication, date, and URL
- Use consistent citation format
- Front matter format:

```
## References

1. Author Name, "Article Title," Publication Name, Date. [URL]
2. Author Name, "Article Title," Publication Name, Date. [URL]
```

## Grammar and Style

### DO: Use Short Sentences

- Most sentences under 15 words for clarity
- Use direct statements
- Prefer active voice

### DO NOT: Use Hyphenated Sentences

- **Never** use em-dashes (—), en-dashes (–), or hyphens to connect clauses
- **Break into separate sentences instead**

### DO NOT: Use Hype Language

**Never use these phrases:**

- "Here's where it gets crazy"
- "This is where it gets interesting"
- "Get this"
- "There is more"

**Instead**: Make points directly. Let the content speak for itself.

### DO NOT: Overuse Signposting Phrases

**Limit to 1-2 per article:**

- "Here's something else most people don't realize"
- "Did you know"
- "Here's the thing"
- "What most people don't realize is"

**These are acceptable for opening hook only. Do not repeat throughout.**

### DO: Use Passive Voice Sparingly

- Prefer active voice
- Only use when agent is unknown or irrelevant

## Content Rules

### DO NOT: Assume Reader Background

- Never assume reader has read the Bible
- Never assume reader knows Christian doctrines or church teaching
- Always provide brief neutral introductions for biblical figures, stories, and theological terms
- Never assume reader accepts existence of God, authority of Scripture, or Church teaching
- Write for atheists, agnostics, spiritual non-believers, and the unchurched

### DO: Present Evidence Properly

- Support all claims with reasons or evidence
- Treat Scripture/doctrine references as evidence to explain, not self-evident truths
- Present evidence first; acknowledge mystery second

### DO: Define Terms Appropriately

- Define terms a general audience would not know
- Skip definitions for common terms: archaeology, inscription, manuscript, excavation, prophecy, resurrection

### DO: Write at National Reading Level

- Use simple, plain English
- Target 6th-7th grade reading level
- Avoid complex vocabulary and sentence structures
- Write like you would speak to someone over coffee
